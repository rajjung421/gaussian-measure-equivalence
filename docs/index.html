When is a Gaussian prior “the same kind of thing”?

Learning notes · Raj Mahat · Georgia Tech

Computational notes on the Feldman–Hájek theorem and what it means for building priors for groundwater heads: which nonstationary features (wells, leakage, a conductivity lens) can be added as a correction to a stationary SPDE prior, and which force the operator itself to change.

Two Gaussian measures are equivalent iff a single, infinitely resolved sample cannot tell them apart. If one perfect sample can, they are mutually singular.

Chapter 1 · Feldman–Hájek and measure equivalence

StepWhat the computation shows
Finite dimensionsNon-degenerate Gaussians are always equivalent; singularity needs a degenerate support.
Infinitely many coordinatesA 20% variance change in every coordinate becomes perfectly detectable from one sample; equivalence holds iff Σ tj2 < ∞ (Kakutani).
Mean shiftsEquivalent iff the shift lies in the Cameron–Martin space; for the SPDE prior that is H², so a point well is singular.
Range vs amplitudeChanging the range κ looks dramatic but is equivalent; changing the amplitude τ looks mild but is singular. One sample pins τ, never κ.
1D leaky aquiferWell and leakage: ‖T‖ flat under refinement. Conductivity lens: ‖T‖ ∝ √N, and one head field identifies it.
